★ RoboChargers · Surge
4x NEO Vortex pivoting launcher with amp diverter; double-sided under-bumper intake; telescoping climber; swerve
54
Local EPA
Yes
USA-TX · 59-10 · #4
Shooter (speaker)
Pivoting launcher with four NEO Vortex motors, chosen for mechanical simplicity: there is no mechanical linkage between the four corners of the launcher. A speed difference between the two sides puts spin on the note. Surgical tubing counterbalances the launcher.
Intake
Under-the-bumper, double-sided intake. The intake was upgraded several times during the season and moved between the competition robot, the practice robot and a spare intake.
Feeder / amp / trap
A diverter on the launcher allows both amp and speaker shots. A team member mentions a feeder motor that was never removed during the season.
Drivetrain
Swerve with NEO Vortex motors. The team rotates used swerve modules into the spares pool so spares are 'known good'. A team member credits SPARK Flex controllers with fewer module and motor swaps this season.
Endgame
Single telescoping climber. One part on the robot keeps notes from getting stuck on the climber.
Auto
Unconfirmed

Unconfirmed
Why study it
Study the top-level robot CAD and the block CAD side by side as a layout-to-detail workflow. Then study the launcher and its SRPP plates. FIRST In Texas District Championship winner and Galileo Division Industrial Design Award.
Lessons for students
  • Block CAD released next to the finished robot: open both Onshape documents to see how the layout model drives each subsystem. The team states that links from the robot to the block CAD cannot be opened by right-click.
  • Four independent launcher motors remove the mechanical linkage between the launcher corners. The team states the same spin could be made without four motors, so the choice was for mechanical simplicity.
  • SRPP as a polycarbonate replacement: the team lists lower density, higher tensile stiffness, Loctite resistance and laser cutting as the reasons. The team also states it gives little to no accuracy benefit over polycarbonate.
  • A spares practice: used swerve modules move into the spares pool so that spares are known to work.
  • The block CAD method is documented in a FIRST Mentor Conference talk by a 3005 member, with video and slides.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. A reveal post that names the main mechanisms, and OP and team-member replies that state launcher motors, materials and drivetrain motors. The block CAD talk at the FIRST Mentor Conference was posted 2024-08-30. Released 2024-04-15 on Chief Delphi.

Shooter (speaker)
Two-sided wheel shooter. It is belted 1:1 off a Kraken, so the motor's internal absolute encoder handles position control. Why study it
A shooter specified in one post, plus a belted half-moon wheel that stops the note, flips up for shots and sends it to the amp elevator. The team added the half-moon because reversing the shooter wheels did not lift the note with 4.5 in wheels.
Lessons for students
  • A shooter specified in one post, with wheel diameter, material, compression, center-to-center, ratio and motor count.
  • Retest a borrowed trick on the new geometry: reversing the shooter wheels lifted notes on the team's turret robot but not with 4.5 in wheels, so the team added a half-moon wheel.

  • Tension belts by reprinting: idlers are toothless pulleys on shoulder bolts, and the team reprints an idler 2-3 times after each spacing or ratio change.
  • Match parts to build speed: the team kept a traditional bearing stack instead of a large X-contact bearing because it builds the robot in under a week after design lock.

★ Orbit · Doppler
Low-profile robot; shooter pivot driven by machined pin-tooth pinions; amp and trap mechanisms; swerve
52
Local EPA
Yes
Israel · 66-5-1 · #5
Shooter (speaker)
Shooter on a pivot. The pivot pinions are machined from two plates with steel pins between them that act as teeth. The shooter is removable: clamshell plates with interlocking 'Lego'-style edges clamp it to custom blocks on the main shaft; the team says this is more rigid than screws alone. Two mechanical stops below the shooter edges hold it in travel mode, and a Hall effect sensor detects the arm at the bottom of its travel. A team member wrote that other teams' spin shooters worked better than Doppler's.
Intake
A team member wrote that other teams' larger-diameter intakes worked better than Doppler's.
Feeder / amp / trap
The robot has an amp mechanism and a trap mechanism. In the trap mechanism a servo pulls a thin 3D-printed lever to release the arm. A constant force spring pulls the arm forward, and a torsion spring in its 3D-printed pivot rotates it. A team member wrote that downward-shooting amp mechanisms, and trap mechanisms that get closer to the trap, worked better than Doppler's.
Drivetrain
Swerve. The team uses an MA3 absolute encoder on its swerve modules.
Endgame
The robot has a trap mechanism with a servo release and spring-driven arm. The robot slows its drive in the climb state.
Auto
The team ran a 7 note auto. Paths come from the team's own Pathfinder tool. It writes a CSV that the robot parses before each match, with velocity and acceleration limits. Note detection lets the robot skip a missing midline note: in the auto run through most of the Championship playoffs, the robot looks for the first midline note; if it is absent, it jumps ahead and drives to the third. Won the Autonomous Award at ISR District Event #1.
Vision
Three cameras. A Limelight at the back (shooter side) and an Arducam running PhotonVision at the front read AprilTags. A second front camera detects notes. The June 2024 software sessions covered pose tracking and shooting on the move. Note positions come from the camera's Tx and Ty with trigonometry. The team scales its own odometry by a carpet factor measured in practice matches: drive the field end to end and compare.
Why study it
Study the low-height packaging: what a low robot gains and what it gives up. A team member wrote that low height was the one thing the team perfected, and that other teams' spin shooters, larger intakes, downward amp mechanisms and trap mechanisms worked better than Doppler's. Championship Winner on Einstein and Daly Division winner.
Lessons for students
  • Pin-tooth pinions drive the pivot; the team says they are less complex to make than they look.
  • Build a fallback into an auto path: when the camera does not see the first midline note, Doppler's auto jumps to a later path point and goes for the third note.
  • Calibrate odometry to the carpet with a factor measured in a practice match.
  • Integrate CAD often: the team defines subsystem volumes first, gives each subsystem a CAD lead, and integrates about 3 times a day.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. Slides from the team's June 2024 software session. Team replies through 2025 explain the shooter mount, the trap release, the cameras and the auto logic. Released 2024-04-29 on Chief Delphi.

★ OP Robotics · LOW-KEY
Shooter on a low-backlash arm pivot; Limelight 3G 2D aiming; pneumatic climber; swerve
52
Local EPA

Canada · 67-3 · #6
Shooter (speaker)
Upper and lower flywheels; the arm sets the shot angle. After the first district event the team tipped the flywheels downward for a lower release angle, so the robot could score from the wing line; the arm could not go low enough for that shot. The feed position did not change, so the note handoff was not affected.
Intake
Rollers of 1 in OD, 3/8 in ID 40A polyurethane stretched onto live 1/2 in rounded hex shafts. Timing pulleys in the middle of the rollers let the timing belts act as conveyor belts. Side kicker rollers are 2 in OD stealth wheels. One Kraken X60 drives the intake through a 2:1 reduction for a linear speed of 25 ft/s.
Feeder / amp / trap
Shooter feed rollers are blue 1 in OD sushi rollers on a 1/2 in hex shaft. The arm drives to an amp scoring position with CTRE Motion Magic and tracks the speaker with a PD loop with no gravity term. The arm gearbox is custom, with every gear bonded to its shaft with Loctite 609.
Drivetrain
Swerve. In a team post of 2024-10-25 on module wiring, CAN runs from the first motor to the encoder, to the second motor, then to the next CAN device. CAN and power wires pass through a cut-out in the top module cover and are soldered to the CANcoder board.
Endgame
The climber uses pneumatic quick exhaust valves, which the team buys from Automation Direct.
Auto
In auto, chassis yaw, tx and distance to the tag correct the odometry estimate. The code uses the WPILib timed framework, not command-based. TBA lists the Autonomous Award at McMaster, Newmarket and the Ontario Science Division.
Vision
Limelight 3G on LimelightOS with no coprocessor. It tracks the center speaker AprilTag in 2D: tx turns the chassis to aim and ty gives distance, which sets the arm angle. No MegaTag or 3D pose estimation. The team states that 2D tracking runs at a higher FPS and needs no filtering or latency compensation.
Why study it
Study the arm gearbox as a backlash-control case. Team replies also explain the arm control, the 2D Limelight 3G aiming and the mid-season shooter change. Galileo Division and Ontario Provincial Championship winner.
Lessons for students
  • Backlash control without shim tape: Loctite 609 fills the gap in each gear bore, and the final stage uses a broached sprocket and a 1 in long machined gear hub instead of a 1/2 in deep gear bore.
  • Change the cheap part first: tipping the flywheels downward lowered the release angle for wing-line shots without a new arm range or a new note handoff.
  • Simple vision can be enough: 2D tracking of one AprilTag aimed the chassis and set the arm angle, with no pose filtering or latency compensation.
  • Match the controller to the move: Motion Magic for large arm moves and a PD loop for tracking a moving setpoint.
  • The team states why it builds custom gearboxes instead of planetaries: custom packaging and chain tensioning, in-house manufacturing, and a way to remove backlash in a long arm.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. Technical binder and CAD drawing package, plus team replies that state parts, ratios, control modes and vision use. A reply from a user outside the team describes the drawing package as 166 pages with assembly instructions and supplier part numbers. Released 2024-05-10 on Chief Delphi.

★ Quixilver · Domotron
Shooter; chain-deployed intake with flexible polycarbonate rollers; elevator; climber
49
Local EPA
Yes
USA-CA · 58-10-1 · #10
Shooter (speaker)
The shooter hex shafts were cut shorter on the robot than the CAD shows.
Intake
Deployable intake with polycarbonate rollers that flex in an impact; a roller could flex more than half its diameter in a bad hit. Short shaft segments replace a full-width hex shaft, which would bend permanently. The left and right sides are tied together through chain and a cross shaft inside the frame perimeter, so no rigid cross bars are used. The team chose 3/8 in bearings for weight and fit in the tube plugs, and used 3/8 in OD spacers in place of hex. The team reports two failure points it did not solve in season: the bolts holding the axles to the side plates sheared, and the deploy chain snapped.
Feeder / amp / trap
The robot has an elevator. Two hex shafts on the elevator came from an earlier climber iteration.
Drivetrain
The bellypan holes were hand-drilled, not CNC-cut, and the CAD omits most of them. Electronics are suppressed in the CAD to reduce lag; the team planned the layout in advance.
Endgame
The robot has a climber. The team reused the top elevator hex shaft to tension the climber with surgical tubing. The tubing is not in the CAD.
Auto
The team won the Autonomous Award in the Milstein Division.
Vision
Unconfirmed
Why study it
Study the intake as a compliance design: polycarbonate rollers flex in a bad hit, and short shaft segments do not bend permanently. The team also lists the failures it did not solve in season: sheared axle bolts and a snapped deploy chain. Read the CAD together with the thread, which states where the built robot differs from the model; Milstein Division winner and Autonomous Award.
Lessons for students
  • Design the intake to flex instead of resisting impact: polycarbonate rollers on short shaft segments can deflect more than half their diameter without bending a shaft.
  • Tie the two sides of a wide intake together through a cross shaft and chain inside the frame perimeter, so the rollers need no rigid cross bars.
  • Record the failure modes you did not fix. The team lists sheared axle bolts and a snapped deploy chain as open problems for a similar future intake.
  • Compare the CAD with the built robot. The team states where they differ: hand-drilled bellypan holes, shorter shooter shafts, and climber tensioning by surgical tubing.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. A walkthrough video. Team replies in the release thread explain the intake design and its failure points. Released 2025-01-01 on Chief Delphi.
